Mission Profiles

Every Orion Nexus mission is designed to operate without human crews — performing critical work in orbit and deep space safely and sustainably.

🚀

Launch & Return

Autonomous pre-launch checks, powered ascent to orbit, payload deployment, and precision vertical landing for rapid reuse.

🛰️

Orbital Operations

Carry payloads, deploy satellites, and perform space missions autonomously before returning to Earth.

🕸️

Debris Capture

Detect, approach, and secure defunct satellites, rocket stages, and fragments using robotic arms, nets, and harpoons.

🛡️

Debris Shielding

Plasma shields, interceptor arms, and magnetic docking protect active satellites and stations from impact threats.

⚡

Kinetic Harvesting

Intercept high-speed boulders, absorb kinetic energy, and process volatile materials into hydrogen fuel and usable resources.

🔭

Deep-Space Exploration

Long-duration planetary survey, sample collection, data transmission, and the search for extraterrestrial life.

Universal Mission Sequence

A shared autonomous workflow that spans nearly every Orion Nexus operation.

01 — Detect

AI scans and identifies targets using 360° LiDAR, radar, optical cameras, and infrared sensors.

02 — Approach

The craft calculates a safe trajectory and autonomously maneuvers to the target position.

03 — Capture

Robotic arms, nets, or harpoons secure the target depending on size, shape, and motion.

04 — Process

Debris is shredded, melted, sorted, refined, or stored for reuse — extracting every possible resource.

05 — Dispose / Return

Non-recyclable waste is safely deorbited or vaporized; the vehicle returns to patrol, land, or prepare for the next mission.
